# Trail Runner Strength, Plyometrics & Tendon Care — Year-Round Plan

**Season-agnostic revision** · Written 2026-08-16 · **Revised 2026-08-17 after evidence review** · **Weekly layout revised 2026-10-22** (Session B → Thursday PM) · Replaces the R2R2R-anchored version (race cancelled — North Rim closure)
**Goal:** durable uphill/downhill trail running strength, built in repeating cycles
**Assumes:** full gym access · right posterior tibial tendon currently quiet · 4 runs/week (Tue/Thu/Sat/Sun)

> **Aug 2026 revision — what changed and why.** Gym ME circuits and weighted uphill carries are removed. Load-matched trials (Li 2020) and meta-analysis (Llanos-Lagos 2024, Sports Med) show heavy (>80% 1RM) and plyometric training improve running economy and performance while submaximal/high-rep work does not; Schoenfeld 2021 found no unique muscular-endurance benefit from high reps; and high-rep gym circuits largely duplicate the stress running already provides (CTS/Pittman 2026). Weighted carries were dropped for injury caution — they were the weakest-evidence element. Muscular endurance is now built **on the hill** via the Thursday rotation (below); the gym stays heavy and crisp, nothing to failure.

## The structure: two alternating blocks, forever

No race date means no taper and no terminal peak. Instead, rotate two block types year-round, separated by deload weeks:

| Block                              | Length    | Purpose                                                                               | Gym emphasis                                                                                                | Plyo dose                            |
| ---------------------------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **MAX — Max Strength**             | 6 weeks   | Raise the force ceiling. Higher ceiling = every trail stride costs a smaller % of max | Heavy barbell work, 80→90% 1RM, low reps                                                                    | Low (~60 contacts, primer only)      |
| **ME — Muscular Endurance + Plyo** | 6–8 weeks | Convert the ceiling into fatigue resistance and elastic descent capacity              | Heavy bilateral + heavy single-leg + full plyo progression; ME itself lives on the hill (Thursday rotation) | High (110–140 contacts, progressing) |

**Cycle rhythm:** MAX (6 wk, deload at wk 4) → deload wk → ME (6–8 wk, deload at wk 4) → deload wk → repeat.
One full cycle ≈ 14–16 weeks; two cycles per year plus a lighter maintenance stretch fits naturally around seasons.

**If a race appears on the calendar:** schedule so an ME block ends 10 days out, then run the Taper Week session (end of this document) once, then race. That's the whole conversion back to a race plan.

## The ordering rules (unchanged — race-independent)

### Within a session — always this sequence

1. **General warm-up** — raise temperature, groove patterns
2. **Activation primers** — glute med + foot intrinsics wake up before anything loads the tendon
3. **Plyometrics** — highest neural demand, lowest fatigue tolerance. Always fresh. The moment landings get loud or slow, you're done
4. **Heavy strength** — maximal force needs a fresh-ish nervous system; tolerates more fatigue than plyos
5. **Heavy single-leg work** — step-ups, split squats, SL RDL at low reps; the unilateral strength descents and climbs actually draw on
6. **Tendon HSR** — slow, low skill demand, safe when tired; done last so it doesn't steal from squats or plyos
7. **Cool-down**

Everything in the gym is heavy and crisp — 2 reps in reserve (RPE 8), nothing chased for burn. Fatigue-seeking circuits are gone: the evidence says they duplicate run stress without adding what the gym uniquely provides.

### Within the week — hard days hard, easy days easy

| Day | Session                                                                                       | Logic                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mon | **Session A**                                                                                 | Non-run day, 48 h after Saturday with an easy Sunday between                                                                                                                 |
| Tue | Easy Z2 run (HR ≤150 · RPE 5–6)                                                               | Cap 150 since the Oct 20 drift test (+3.8% Pa:HR at HR 155)                                                                                                                  |
| Wed | Easy Z2 run                                                                                   | Second easy day — Thursday starts on fresh legs                                                                                                                              |
| Thu | Uphill intervals AM — rotation: SteadyState → Tempo → VO2 (see below) — then **Session B** PM | Hard day hard: the week's only intensity and the single-leg lift share one day, so the days on either side stay easy. Deload weeks: easy run + strides, then Session B light |
| Fri | Rest + 5-min tendon micro-dose only                                                           | Absorption day before the weekend                                                                                                                                            |
| Sat | Long mountain run                                                                             | ~48 h after the intervals, ~42 h after Session B — Z2 with an HR cap, the session least hurt by leftover soreness                                                            |
| Sun | Easy B2B run                                                                                  | —                                                                                                                                                                            |

Same-day run + lift: **run first, lift second, ≥6 h apart** (one window only → lift straight after the cool-down). Never lift heavy the day before the Saturday long run. Session B leaves 2–3 reps in reserve; ahead of a big Saturday descent, swap its slow-eccentric step-downs for step-ups — the weekend's descents supply that dose.

**Why Session B left Wednesday (revised 2026-10-22):** lower-body lifting — the eccentric single-leg work most of all — leaves the legs sore and less economical for one to three days, less so once lifting is habitual. On Wednesday that window landed on the week's only quality session, and on both benchmark retests. Stacking it after Thursday's intervals keeps the soreness off anything that needs to be fast.

**When Sunday is the long run** (skimo season: Saturday skin, Sunday long): Session A moves to Tuesday PM after the easy run and Monday becomes rest or an easy spin — plyos and depth drops need legs 48 h off the long run, not 24.

**Run volume policy (added Aug 2026):** resume at ~45 mpw, build **≤10% per build week to 50 mpw**, then hold. Deload weeks run ~30 (60%). The extra miles come from a 5th weekly run — an easy 6-miler Wednesday — and longer Sundays, not a bigger Saturday. The 10% cap is a ceiling, not a target (Buist 2008 found no injury protection from strict 10% grading; the real governors are ACWR ~0.8–1.3 and the tendon's 24-h rule). Volume holds at 50 through MAX blocks: concurrent-training interference (Wilson 2012) costs strength gains, not running — running is the priority, so the bar moves slower and that's accepted.

### Shared components (identical in every session, both blocks)

**Warm-up (8 min, flow continuously):** bike/row 1 × 3 min · leg swings fwd/back 1 × 10 each leg · leg swings side/side 1 × 10 each leg · hip circles 1 × 10 each direction each leg · walking lunge w/ rotation 1 × 10 · A-skip 2 × 30 m (walk back). No rest between items.

**Primers (4 min):** lateral band walk 2 × 12 each direction (30 sec rest) · foot doming 1 × 10 each foot, 5-sec holds (no rest).

**Cool-down (5 min):** easy spin/walk 1 × 3 min · calf stretch straight-knee 1 × 30 sec each · calf stretch bent-knee 1 × 30 sec each · couch stretch 1 × 30 sec each. No rest between stretches.

# THURSDAY UPHILL QUALITY — THE ROTATION

One uphill interval session per week, rotating through three stimuli in order across build weeks (deload weeks: easy run instead). All three are interval-based, following the CTS/Koop workout structure (Koop, "Decoding Interval Workouts"): interval length, total time-at-intensity, and work:rest ratio define the session — pace yourself to the highest effort you can sustain for the _entire set_.

**Effort anchors (RPE, 1–10, CTS run scale):** recovery = 4–5 · endurance/easy = 5–6 ("all day" pace, full sentences) · SteadyState = 7 (sub-LT) · Tempo = 8–9 (LT) · VO2 intervals = 10. Lifting uses its own scale: gym RPE 8 = 2 reps in reserve. When HR and RPE disagree (heat, fatigue, altitude), trust RPE and investigate why.

**1. Uphill SteadyState** — _high-end aerobic (RPE 7 · sub-threshold · 5–8:1 work:rest)_

- Warm up 15–20 min easy → **2 × 20 min at RPE 7** (build to 2 × 25 min) on a 4–8% sustained grade → 4–5 min jog-down recovery → cool down
- Above endurance pace, below lactate threshold — "strong but sustainable." This is an interval workout, not a Z2 jog: it should feel like work
- The discipline is the ceiling, not the floor: don't let the grade drag RPE 7 into 8. Total time-at-intensity 40–50 min

**2. Uphill Tempo** — _lactate threshold (RPE 8–9 · 2:1 work:rest)_

- Warm up 15–20 min easy → **3 × 10 min at RPE 8–9** on a 6–10% sustained grade → ~5-min jog-down recoveries → cool down 10 min
- Short answers, hard breathing. Raises fractional utilization; total time-at-intensity capped at ~30 min to keep the week pyramidal

**3. Uphill VO2** — _the engine (RPE 10 · 1:1 work:rest · strongest trial evidence: Barnes 2013, Ferley 2013)_

- Warm up 20 min easy → **5 × 3 min at RPE 10** on a 10–15% grade → 3-min jog-down recoveries → cool down
- 2–4 min reps are the VO2max sweet spot — shorter never reaches VO2max, longer forces under-pacing (Seiler & Sjursen 2004)
- Max _repeatable_ effort: pace to finish rep 5 at the same quality as rep 1, not to win rep 1

Easy Z2 climbing volume hasn't disappeared — it lives in the Saturday long mountain runs, where most weekly vert accumulates at RPE 5–6. The Thursday slot is now purely a quality slot. Weighted carries remain retired (injury caution; weakest evidence base).

## The plyometric ladder (advance by criteria, not calendar)

**Advancement rule:** minimum 2 weeks per level. Move up only when (a) tendon at baseline the morning after every session at the current level, and (b) landings still crisp in the final set. Flare = drop one level for a week. During MAX blocks, hold at primer dose regardless of level achieved — the level is banked, not lost.

Rest: **90 sec between sets of bilateral drills; 2 min between sets of depth drops and all single-leg drills.** Flat, forgiving surface. Stop any set that gets loud, slow, or unstable.

| Level                     | Prescription (Session A, slot 3)                                                                                                                                                                                       | Contacts          |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| **L1 — Re-establish**     | Pogo hops 3 × 15 (90 s) · low box jump w/ step-down 4 × 5 (90 s) · lateral line hops 2 × 15 (90 s)                                                                                                                     | ~110              |
| **L2 — Single-leg**       | SL pogos 3 × 10 each leg (2 min) · box jump 4 × 5 (90 s) · lateral bound to stick 3 × 5 each side (2 min)                                                                                                              | ~110              |
| **L3 — Depth intro**      | SL pogos 3 × 12 each leg (2 min) · depth drop to stick, 30 cm 4 × 5 (2 min) · lateral bounds 3 × 6 each side (2 min)                                                                                                   | ~130              |
| **L4 — Descent-specific** | Depth drops, 40 cm 4 × 5 (2 min) · SL lateral hops 3 × 8 each leg (2 min) · bounding 3 × 20 m (2 min)                                                                                                                  | ~140              |
| **L5 — Sport transfer**   | Keep L4 in the gym at reduced volume (depth drops 3 × 5, SL lateral hops 2 × 8 each leg, 2 min rests) **and add downhill strides to Thursday's run: 5 × 25 sec on a −5 to −8% smooth grade, jog-up recovery (~2 min)** | ~90 gym + strides |

L5 is the destination: gym-built elasticity expressed at running speed on real descents. Earn it — it's also the highest tendon demand in the program.

## Posterior tibial tendon: permanent maintenance

Symmetry normalized (51.2% → 50.65% L/R stance balance) and symptoms are quiet. This is now **standing infrastructure, not rehab** — run load will keep rising and tendon capacity must stay ahead of it.

**1. Heavy load, 2×/week (in-session, both blocks):** weighted SL heel raise 4 × 6 in Session A + bodyweight 2 × 15 in Session B. **After 6 consecutive symptom-free months you may drop Session B's set — never Session A's.** While you run mountains, the heavy dose never goes to zero.

**2. Daily micro-dose (5 min, with morning coffee, no rest between items):**

| Exercise                        | Sets × reps                    |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Foot doming, 5-sec holds        | 1 × 10 each foot               |
| Toe yoga                        | 1 × 10 each pattern, each foot |
| Resisted inversion, band, slow  | 1 × 15 each foot               |
| Single-leg balance, eyes closed | 1 × 30 sec each leg            |
| Calf stretch, straight knee     | 1 × 30 sec each side           |
| Calf stretch, bent knee         | 1 × 30 sec each side           |

**3. Pre-run (2 min, every run):** ankle circles 1 × 10 each direction each foot · band inversion 1 × 15 each foot · bodyweight heel raise 1 × 10. No rest between items.

**Running cues:** easy-run cadence 175+ spm (it drifted to 171 — set a watch alert) · half-shoe-width wider stance on technical descents · poles on any descent over ~2,000 ft.

**Regression ladder — if morning pain sits above baseline >24 h:**

1. **Cut plyometrics first** — drop entirely for 7 days, keep everything else. Highest tendon strain per minute
2. Still elevated after 3 days → reduce HSR load 20–30%, keep frequency (still 4 × 6, 2 min rest)
3. Still elevated → pause all dynamic calf work; replace with isometric holds **5 × 30 sec at 80% effort, 60 sec rest, 1–2×/day**
4. Sharp pain, gait change, swelling, or any pop/snap → stop; see the PT before the next session

**Running is never the first thing cut** while symptoms are ≤2/10 and resolving within 24 h.

## Taper Week (only when racing — file away until needed)

One session, 10–12 days before race day (~30 min): Warm-up as above → pogo hops 2 × 10, 60 sec rest → back squat 2 × 3 @ 65% 1RM, fast and crisp, 3 min rest → SL heel raise weighted 3 × 6 each leg @ 80% normal load, 2 min rest → cool-down. Daily micro-dose continues through two days before the race. Nothing to failure, nothing new.
